# Standing Wave Modes: Calculating Wavelength

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Nodes and Antinodes
In a standing wave on a string fixed at both ends, the wave consists of stationary points and points of maximum movement:
* **Nodes:** Points that do not move at all (zero amplitude).
* **Antinodes:** The "bellies" or points of maximum amplitude. 



### 2. The Geometry of a Standing Wave
Each "belly" (one antinode to the next node) represents exactly **half of a wavelength** ($\lambda/2$). Therefore, the total length of the string ($L$) is equal to the number of antinodes ($n$) multiplied by half a wavelength.

$$
L = n \left( \frac{\lambda}{2} \right)
$$

### 3. Wavelength Formula for the $n$-th Harmonic
By rearranging the geometric relationship, we can directly find the wavelength ($\lambda$) if we know the length of the string and the number of antinodes (which also corresponds to the harmonic number $n$).

$$
\lambda = \frac{2L}{n}
$$

---

## Problem Statement

A standing wave with four antinodes is produced on a string of length L = 80 cm. What is the wavelength of this wave?

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Identify the Known Variables
* **Length of the string ($L$):** 80 cm
* **Number of antinodes ($n$):** 4

### Step 2: Set up the geometric relationship
We know that 4 antinodes mean there are 4 "half-waves" fitting perfectly into the 80 cm string. Mathematically, this is written as:

$$
L = 4 \left( \frac{\lambda}{2} \right)
$$

### Step 3: Solve for the Wavelength ($\lambda$)
Use the rearranged wavelength formula for the 4th harmonic ($n = 4$):

$$
\lambda = \frac{2L}{4}
$$

Substitute the length of the string ($L = 80$ cm):

$$
\lambda = \frac{2 \cdot 80}{4}
$$

$$
\lambda = \frac{160}{4}
$$

$$
\lambda = 40 \text{ cm}
$$

*Explanation:* A full wave consists of two antinodes (one positive peak and one negative peak). If 4 antinodes fit into the total 80 cm space, then 2 antinodes (which make up exactly one full wavelength) will fit into exactly half of that distance, which is 40 cm.

---

## Final Results Summary

* **Length of the string ($L$):** 80 cm
* **Number of antinodes ($n$):** 4
* **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** 40 cm (or 0.4 m)
