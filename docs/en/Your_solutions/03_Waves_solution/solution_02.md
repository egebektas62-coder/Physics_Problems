# String Harmonics: Speed of a Wave on a Guitar String

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Standing Waves on a String
When a string is plucked (like a guitar string), it vibrates and creates a standing wave. The ends of the string are fixed, meaning they cannot move. These fixed points are called **Nodes**. The points of maximum vibration are called **Antinodes**.



### 2. Fundamental Frequency (First Harmonic)
The fundamental frequency is the lowest frequency at which the string vibrates. In this mode, the string forms exactly one antinode in the middle. Because a full wave consists of two "bellies" (two antinodes), a string with only one antinode represents exactly half of a full wavelength ($\lambda$).

$$
L = \frac{\lambda}{2} \implies \lambda = 2L
$$

### 3. Wave Speed ($v$)
The speed of the wave traveling through the string is determined by multiplying its frequency ($f$) by its wavelength ($\lambda$).

$$
v = f \cdot \lambda
$$

---

## Problem Statement

A guitar string is 64 cm long and has a fundamental frequency (one antinode) of 330 Hz. What is the speed of the wave on this string?

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Convert units to standard SI units
Before starting calculations, all measurements must be in standard units (meters for length). 
* **Length ($L$):** $64 \text{ cm} = 0.64 \text{ m}$
* **Frequency ($f$):** $330 \text{ Hz}$

### Step 2: Determine the Wavelength ($\lambda$)
Since the problem specifies the "fundamental frequency (one antinode)", the physical string represents exactly half of a full wave. To find the total length of one full wave, we must double the string's length.

$$
\lambda = 2L
$$

Substitute the length of the string:

$$
\lambda = 2 \cdot 0.64
$$

$$
\lambda = 1.28 \text{ m}
$$

*Explanation:* Even though the string is only 64 cm long, the full physical length of the standing wave it produces is 1.28 meters.

### Step 3: Calculate the Wave Speed ($v$)
Now that we have both the frequency and the wavelength, we can find how fast the wave travels back and forth along the guitar string.

$$
v = f \cdot \lambda
$$

Substitute the known values:

$$
v = 330 \cdot 1.28
$$

$$
v = 422.4 \text{ m/s}
$$

*Explanation:* The mechanical wave propagates along the guitar string at an impressive speed of 422.4 meters per second.

---

## Final Results Summary

* **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** $1.28 \text{ m}$
* **Speed of the wave ($v$):** $422.4 \text{ m/s}$
