# Echo Ranging: Calculating Distance

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Echo and Reflection
An echo is a reflection of sound that arrives at the listener with a delay after the direct sound. For an echo to be heard, the sound wave must travel from the source to the reflecting surface (like a cliff) and then bounce back to the source.



### 2. Distance, Speed, and Time
For a wave traveling at a constant speed, the total distance covered is the product of its speed and the total time elapsed.

$$
d_{total} = v \cdot t
$$

### 3. The "Round Trip" Factor
Since the sound has to travel to the cliff and back, the total distance the sound travels is twice the actual distance ($D$) to the cliff.

$$
2D = v \cdot t \implies D = \frac{v \cdot t}{2}
$$

---

## Problem Statement

A person shouts towards a cliff and hears the echo 1 seconds later. How far away is the cliff? (Speed of sound in air is 343 m/s).

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Identify the Known Variables
* **Speed of sound ($v$):** 343 m/s
* **Total time for the echo ($t$):** 1 s

### Step 2: Set up the Echo Equation
We know the sound travels to the cliff and back. Let $D$ be the distance to the cliff. The total distance covered by the sound wave is $2D$. 

Using the basic kinematics equation:

$$
2D = v \cdot t
$$

### Step 3: Solve for the Distance ($D$)
Rearrange the formula to isolate $D$:

$$
D = \frac{v \cdot t}{2}
$$

Substitute the given values into the equation:

$$
D = \frac{343 \cdot 1}{2}
$$

Calculate the result:

$$
D = \frac{343}{2}
$$

$$
D = 171.5 \text{ m}
$$

*Explanation:* The sound wave traveled a total of 343 meters in 1 second. However, because half of that journey was spent reaching the cliff and the other half returning, the cliff is exactly half that distance away.

---

## Final Results Summary

* **Total distance traveled by sound:** $343 \text{ m}$
* **Actual distance to the cliff ($D$):** $171.5 \text{ m}$
