# Wave Properties: Sound in Air and Water

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Wave Speed ($v$)
The speed at which the wave propagates through a medium. It depends entirely on the physical properties of the medium (e.g., density, temperature).

### 2. Frequency ($f$)
The number of wave cycles that pass a given point per second. Measured in Hertz (Hz). 
**Crucial Rule:** When a wave passes from one medium to another (e.g., from air to water), its frequency remains *constant*. It is the "signature" of the source.

### 3. Wavelength ($\lambda$)
The physical distance between two consecutive corresponding points on a wave (like peak to peak). 

### The Universal Wave Equation
The relationship between speed, frequency, and wavelength is given by:

$$
v = f \cdot \lambda
$$

To solve for wavelength ($\lambda$), we rearrange the formula:

$$
\lambda = \frac{v}{f}
$$

---

## Problem Statement

A sound wave in air has a frequency of 440 Hz. If the speed of sound in air is 343 m/s, what is its wavelength? What is its wavelength in water, where the speed of sound is 1482 m/s?

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Identify the Known Variables
* **Frequency ($f$):** 440 Hz *(This will remain the same in both air and water)*
* **Speed in Air ($v_{air}$):** 343 m/s
* **Speed in Water ($v_{water}$):** 1482 m/s

### Step 2: Calculate the Wavelength in Air ($\lambda_{air}$)
Using our rearranged wave equation, we plug in the speed of sound in air and the frequency.

$$
\lambda_{air} = \frac{v_{air}}{f}
$$

Substitute the given values:

$$
\lambda_{air} = \frac{343}{440}
$$

$$
\lambda_{air} \approx 0.7795 \text{ m}
$$

*Explanation:* In the air, the physical distance between the sound wave peaks is about 78 centimeters.

### Step 3: Calculate the Wavelength in Water ($\lambda_{water}$)
Now, the sound wave enters the water. The speed increases significantly, but the frequency ($f = 440 \text{ Hz}$) stays exactly the same. 

$$
\lambda_{water} = \frac{v_{water}}{f}
$$

Substitute the water values:

$$
\lambda_{water} = \frac{1482}{440}
$$

$$
\lambda_{water} \approx 3.368 \text{ m}
$$

*Explanation:* Because sound travels much faster in water, the wave is "stretched out" spatially. The distance between the peaks is now almost 3.4 meters.

---

## Final Results Summary

* **Wavelength in Air:** $\approx 0.78 \text{ m}$
* **Wavelength in Water:** $\approx 3.37 \text{ m}$
