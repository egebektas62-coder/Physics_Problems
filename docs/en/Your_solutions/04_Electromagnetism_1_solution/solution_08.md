# Lorentz Force: Moving Charge in a Magnetic Field

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### 1. The Magnetic Lorentz Force
When a charged particle moves through a magnetic field, it experiences a force known as the magnetic Lorentz force. This force is unique because it is always perpendicular to both the particle's velocity vector ($\vec{v}$) and the magnetic field vector ($\vec{B}$).

The magnitude of this force is given by the cross product formula:

$$
F_B = |q| v B \sin(\theta)
$$

Where:
* **$F_B$**: Magnitude of the magnetic force (in Newtons, N)
* **$q$**: Electric charge of the particle (in Coulombs, C)
* **$v$**: Speed of the particle (in m/s)
* **$B$**: Strength of the magnetic field (in Teslas, T)
* **$\theta$**: The angle between the velocity vector and the magnetic field vector.



### 2. The "Perpendicular" Condition
If the particle enters the magnetic field perpendicularly (at a $90^\circ$ angle), then $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$. The equation simplifies to its maximum possible value:

$$
F_B = |q| v B
$$

---

## Problem Statement

A charged particle with charge $q = 2 \times 10^{-19}$ C and mass $m = 4 \times 10^{-27}$ kg enters a magnetic field of $B = 0.5$ T at a speed of $v = 10^6$ m/s perpendicular to the field. What is the magnitude of the Lorentz force acting on the particle?

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Identify the Known Variables
* **Charge ($q$):** $2 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* **Mass ($m$):** $4 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$ *(Note: Mass is given as a distractor; it is not needed to find the force, though it would be needed to find the resulting acceleration or orbital radius).*
* **Magnetic Field ($B$):** $0.5 \text{ T}$
* **Speed ($v$):** $10^6 \text{ m/s}$
* **Angle ($\theta$):** $90^\circ$ (perpendicular)

### Step 2: Set Up the Equation
Since the particle's velocity is perpendicular to the magnetic field, we use the simplified Lorentz force equation:

$$
F_B = q \cdot v \cdot B
$$

### Step 3: Substitute and Calculate
Plug the given values into the equation:

$$
F_B = (2 \times 10^{-19}) \cdot (10^6) \cdot (0.5)
$$

Group the real numbers and the exponents to make the calculation easier:

$$
F_B = (2 \cdot 0.5) \times (10^{-19} \cdot 10^6)
$$

$$
F_B = (1) \times 10^{-19 + 6}
$$

$$
F_B = 1 \times 10^{-13} \text{ N}
$$

*Explanation:* The magnetic field exerts a force of $1 \times 10^{-13}$ Newtons on the particle. This force will not speed up or slow down the particle, but it will constantly change its direction, pushing it into a circular trajectory.

---

## Final Results Summary

* **Magnitude of the Lorentz force ($F_B$):** $1 \times 10^{-13} \text{ N}$
