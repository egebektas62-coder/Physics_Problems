# Field Levitation: Anti-Gravity with Electric Fields

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Gravitational Force
The downward pull of gravity on any particle with mass $m$ near the Earth's surface.

$$
F_g = m \cdot g
$$

### 2. Electric Force in a Uniform Field
When a charge $q$ is placed in an electric field $E$, it experiences an electric force. If the charge is positive (like a proton), the force is in the exact same direction as the electric field.

$$
F_e = q \cdot E
$$

### 3. Levitation (Static Equilibrium)
For an object to levitate, the net force acting on it must be zero. This means the upward electric force must perfectly cancel out the downward gravitational force.

$$
F_e = F_g \implies q \cdot E = m \cdot g
$$

---

## Problem Statement

What electric field strength is required to make a proton levitate against Earth's gravity at the level of the Earth's surface? (Mass of proton $m_p \approx 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$, charge of proton $e \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$, gravitational acceleration $g \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$).

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Set up the Equilibrium Equation
To suspend the proton in mid-air, the upward electric force ($F_e$) must equal the downward gravitational force ($F_g$).

$$
F_e = F_g
$$

Substitute the respective formulas:

$$
e \cdot E = m_p \cdot g
$$

### Step 2: Isolate the Electric Field ($E$)
Rearrange the equation to solve for the electric field strength:

$$
E = \frac{m_p \cdot g}{e}
$$

### Step 3: Substitute the Known Values
Plug in the given physical constants for a proton and Earth's gravity:
* $m_p = 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$
* $g = 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$
* $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$

$$
E = \frac{(1.67 \times 10^{-27}) \cdot 9.8}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}
$$

Calculate the numerator (the gravitational force):

$$
F_g \approx 16.366 \times 10^{-27} \text{ N}
$$

Now divide by the charge of the proton:

$$
E = \frac{16.366 \times 10^{-27}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}
$$

Use exponent rules ($-27 - (-19) = -8$):

$$
E \approx 10.228 \times 10^{-8} \text{ N/C}
$$

Adjust to standard scientific notation:

$$
E \approx 1.02 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N/C} \quad (\text{or V/m})
$$

*Explanation:* Since the proton has a positive charge, the electric field must point directly **upwards** to push the proton up against gravity. The required field strength is incredibly small because the proton's mass is microscopically tiny compared to its charge.

---

## Final Results Summary

* **Required Electric Field Strength ($E$):** $\approx 1.02 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N/C}$
* **Direction of the Field:** Upwards (away from the Earth's center)
