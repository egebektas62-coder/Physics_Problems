# Refraction and Snell's Law

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

Before we solve the problem, let's establish the key concepts regarding the bending of light as it transitions between different materials.

### 1. Index of Refraction ($n$)
The index of refraction of a material is a dimensionless number that describes how fast light travels through that material. It is defined as the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum ($c$) to the phase velocity of light in the medium ($v$).

$$
n = \frac{c}{v}
$$

### 2. Snell's Law (Law of Refraction)
Snell's Law provides the mathematical relationship between the angles of incidence and refraction when light passes through a boundary between two different isotropic media (like air and glass).

$$
n_1 \cdot \sin(\theta_1) = n_2 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)
$$

Where:
*   **$n_1$**: Refractive index of the first medium (where the light originates).
*   **$\theta_1$**: Angle of incidence (the angle between the incident ray and the normal line perpendicular to the surface).
*   **$n_2$**: Refractive index of the second medium.
*   **$\theta_2$**: Angle of refraction (the angle between the refracted ray and the normal line).

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

Let's extract the known variables from the problem statement:
*   **Refractive index of medium 1 (Air), $n_1$:** $1.00$
*   **Refractive index of medium 2 (Glass), $n_2$:** $1.50$
*   **Angle of incidence, $\theta_1$:** $30^\circ$

**Goal:** Find the angle of refraction, $\theta_2$.

### Step 1: Set up Snell's Law
Write down the base equation and substitute the known values into it.

$$
n_1 \cdot \sin(\theta_1) = n_2 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)
$$

$$
1.00 \cdot \sin(30^\circ) = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)
$$

### Step 2: Evaluate the known trigonometric function
We know from standard trigonometry that the sine of $30^\circ$ is exactly $0.5$.

$$
1.00 \cdot 0.5 = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)
$$

$$
0.5 = 1.50 \cdot \sin(\theta_2)
$$

### Step 3: Isolate $\sin(\theta_2)$
Divide both sides of the equation by $1.50$ to solve for the sine of the refraction angle.

$$
\sin(\theta_2) = \frac{0.5}{1.50}
$$

$$
\sin(\theta_2) = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.3333
$$

### Step 4: Calculate the inverse sine ($\arcsin$)
To find the actual angle $\theta_2$, we take the inverse sine of $0.3333$. Make sure your calculator is set to degrees.

$$
\theta_2 = \arcsin(0.3333)
$$

$$
\theta_2 \approx 19.47^\circ
$$

---

## Final Result

The angle of refraction as the light enters the glass is approximately **$19.47^\circ$**. 

*(Note: The light ray bends towards the normal because it is traveling from a medium with a lower refractive index to a medium with a higher refractive index).*
