# Magnetic Torque on a Rectangular Loop

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Magnetic Dipole Moment
A current-carrying loop creates a magnetic dipole. The magnitude of this dipole moment ($\mu$) is the product of the current ($I$) and the area ($A$) of the loop.

$$
\mu = I \cdot A
$$

### 2. Magnetic Torque
The torque ($\tau$) exerted on a magnetic dipole in a uniform magnetic field is given by the cross product of the dipole moment vector and the magnetic field vector.

$$
\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu} \times \vec{B}
$$

$$
\tau = \mu B \sin(\theta)
$$

Where **$\theta$** is the angle between the **normal** to the loop (the direction of $\vec{\mu}$) and the magnetic field.

---

## Problem Statement

A rectangular loop of wire with dimensions $10 \text{ cm}$ by $5 \text{ cm}$ carries a current of $2 \text{ A}$. A uniform magnetic field of $B = 0.3 \text{ T}$ is applied parallel to the plane of the loop. What is the magnitude of the magnetic torque on the loop?

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Identify the Known Variables
* **Dimensions:** $l = 0.1 \text{ m}$, $w = 0.05 \text{ m}$
* **Area ($A$):** $0.1 \cdot 0.05 = 0.005 \text{ m}^2$
* **Current ($I$):** $2 \text{ A}$
* **Magnetic Field ($B$):** $0.3 \text{ T}$
* **Orientation:** Field is *parallel* to the plane of the loop.

### Step 2: Determine the Angle ($\theta$)
The normal vector (which defines the direction of the area and the dipole moment $\vec{\mu}$) is perfectly perpendicular to the plane of the loop. Because the magnetic field is flowing parallel to the plane itself, the angle between the normal vector and the magnetic field is $90^\circ$.

$$
\theta = 90^\circ \implies \sin(90^\circ) = 1
$$

### Step 3: Calculate the Torque
Substitute the values into the full torque formula:

$$
\tau = (I \cdot A) \cdot B \cdot \sin(\theta)
$$

$$
\tau = (2 \cdot 0.005) \cdot 0.3 \cdot 1
$$

$$
\tau = (0.01) \cdot 0.3
$$

$$
\tau = 0.003 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}
$$

---

## Final Results Summary

* **Magnitude of the magnetic torque ($\tau$):** $0.003 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$
