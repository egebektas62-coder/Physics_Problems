# Speed of Light in Media: Diamond

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

Before calculating the speed of light in a specific material, let's review the fundamental concepts regarding light propagation in different media.

### 1. Speed of Light in a Vacuum ($c$)
The speed of light in a perfect vacuum is a universal physical constant. It represents the absolute speed limit of the universe.
*   **$c \approx 3.00 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$**

### 2. Index of Refraction ($n$)
The index of refraction (or refractive index) is a dimensionless number that describes how fast light travels through a specific material compared to how fast it travels in a vacuum. Because light always travels slower in a material than in a vacuum, $n$ is always greater than or equal to 1.

$$
n = \frac{c}{v}
$$

Where:
*   **$n$**: Index of refraction of the medium.
*   **$c$**: Speed of light in a vacuum.
*   **$v$**: Speed of light in the specific medium.

To find the speed of light in the medium ($v$), we rearrange the formula:

$$
v = \frac{c}{n}
$$

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

Let's extract the known variables from the problem statement and apply our formula.

### Step 1: Identify the Known Variables
*   **Speed of light in a vacuum ($c$):** $3.00 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$
*   **Refractive index of diamond ($n$):** $2.42$

### Step 2: Set Up the Equation
Using the rearranged formula for the speed of light in a medium, substitute the known values.

$$
v = \frac{3.00 \times 10^8}{2.42}
$$

### Step 3: Calculate the Speed ($v$)
Perform the division ($3.00 \div 2.42$). 

$$
v \approx 1.2396 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}
$$

Rounding to three significant figures (to match the precision of the given index of refraction, $2.42$):

$$
v \approx 1.24 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}
$$

---

## Final Result

The speed of light in a diamond is approximately **$1.24 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$**. 

*(Note: This extremely high refractive index is why light travels so slowly through a diamond, causing the severe bending (refraction) of light and the intense internal reflections that give diamonds their characteristic "sparkle".)*
