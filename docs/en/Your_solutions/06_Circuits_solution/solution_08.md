# AC Voltage Equation in a Resistive Circuit

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### Ohm's Law in AC Circuits
For a purely resistive alternating current (AC) circuit, Ohm's law applies directly to the instantaneous values of voltage and current at any given time t:

$$
V(t) = I(t) \cdot R
$$

### Phase Relationship in Resistors
In a purely resistive circuit, the voltage and current are perfectly in phase. This means their angular frequency remains exactly the same, and there is no phase shift between them.

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

Given parameters from the problem:
* Instantaneous Current: $I(t) = 2 \sin(120 \pi t)$
* Resistance: $R = 50 \ \Omega$

### Step 1: Calculate the Peak Voltage
The peak voltage ($V_{max}$) is the product of the peak current ($I_{max} = 2\ \text{A}$) and the resistance ($R = 50\ \Omega$):

$$
V_{max} = 2 \cdot 50 = 100\ \text{V}
$$

### Step 2: Write the Final Voltage Equation
Since the voltage is in phase with the current, it shares the exact same sinusoidal time-dependence. We combine the calculated peak voltage with the original angular frequency component:

$$
V(t) = 100 \sin(120 \pi t)\ \text{V}
$$

---

## Final Result

$$
V(t) = 100 \sin(120 \pi t)\ \text{V}
$$
