# Transformer Turns Ratio

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### Transformer Voltage Equation
The ratio of the secondary voltage ($V_s$) to the primary voltage ($V_p$) is equal to the ratio of the number of turns in the secondary coil ($N_s$) to the number of turns in the primary coil ($N_p$).

$$
\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}
$$

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

Let's identify the given parameters from the problem:
* **Primary Voltage ($V_p$):** $120\ \text{V}$
* **Secondary Voltage ($V_s$):** $9.0\ \text{V}$
* **Primary Turns ($N_p$):** $400$

### Step 1: Rearrange the formula
We need to find the number of secondary turns ($N_s$). Rearrange the transformer equation to solve for $N_s$:

$$
N_s = N_p \cdot \left( \frac{V_s}{V_p} \right)
$$

### Step 2: Substitute values and calculate
Plug the given values into the rearranged equation:

$$
N_s = 400 \cdot \left( \frac{9.0}{120} \right)
$$

First, simplify the voltage ratio:

$$
\frac{9.0}{120} = 0.075
$$

Now, multiply by the primary turns:

$$
N_s = 400 \cdot 0.075
$$

**$N_s = 30$**

---

## Final Result

The secondary coil must have **$30$ turns**.
