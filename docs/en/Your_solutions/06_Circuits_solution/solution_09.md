# Current from Charge Function

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### Electric Current ($I$)
Electric current is defined as the rate of flow of electric charge over time. Mathematically, it is the first derivative of the charge function $Q(t)$ with respect to time $t$:

$$
I(t) = \frac{dQ(t)}{dt}
$$

Where:
* $I(t)$: Instantaneous current at time $t$ (in Amperes, A)
* $Q(t)$: Charge as a function of time (in Coulombs, C)
* $t$: Time (in seconds, s)

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

Let's identify the given parameters from the problem:
* **Charge function:** $Q(t) = 5t^2 + 5$
* **Specific time:** $t = 3\ \text{s}$

### Step 1: Find the current function $I(t)$
To find the equation for current, we need to take the derivative of the charge function $Q(t)$ with respect to time $t$.

$$
I(t) = \frac{d}{dt} (5t^2 + 5)
$$

Using the power rule for derivatives ($\frac{d}{dt} at^n = n \cdot at^{n-1}$) and knowing the derivative of a constant is zero:

$$
I(t) = 2 \cdot 5t^{2-1} + 0
$$

$$
I(t) = 10t
$$

### Step 2: Calculate the current at $t = 3\ \text{s}$
Now, substitute the specific time $t = 3$ into our instantaneous current function $I(t)$.

$$
I(3) = 10 \cdot (3)
$$

$$
I(3) = 30\ \text{A}
$$

---

## Final Result

The current flowing through the wire at $t = 3\ \text{s}$ is **$30\ \text{A}$**.
