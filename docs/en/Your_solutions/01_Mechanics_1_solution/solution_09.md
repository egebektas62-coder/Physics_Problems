# 9. Momentum Comparison

## Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### 1. Momentum ($p$)
In physics, momentum is the quantity of motion an object possesses. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction, though in this problem we are only comparing magnitudes. It is directly proportional to both an object's mass and its velocity.



The formula for momentum is:

$$
p = m \cdot v
$$

Where:
* **$p$**: Momentum (measured in $\text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}$)
* **$m$**: Mass of the object (must be in kilograms, $\text{kg}$)
* **$v$**: Velocity of the object (must be in meters per second, $\text{m/s}$)

### 2. Unit Conversions
To get standard SI units for momentum, masses given in grams must be converted to kilograms. There are 1000 grams in 1 kilogram:

$$
\text{Mass in kg} = \frac{\text{Mass in g}}{1000}
$$

---

## Problem Statement

Which has greater momentum: a 2-gram fly flying at 10 m/s or a 60-gram tennis ball moving at 1 m/s?

---

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Convert all masses to standard SI units (kilograms)
Before calculating, we must ensure our units are consistent. 

**Mass of the fly ($m_{fly}$):**

$$
m_{fly} = \frac{2}{1000} = 0.002 \text{ kg}
$$

**Mass of the tennis ball ($m_{ball}$):**

$$
m_{ball} = \frac{60}{1000} = 0.060 \text{ kg}
$$

### Step 2: Calculate the momentum of the fly
Use the momentum formula $p = m \cdot v$. We know the fly's velocity is $v_{fly} =$ 10 m/s.

$$
p_{fly} = 0.002 \cdot 10
$$

$$
p_{fly} = 0.02 \text{ kg} \cdot \text{m/s}
$$

### Step 3: Calculate the momentum of the tennis ball
We know the tennis ball's velocity is $v_{ball} =$ 1 m/s.

$$
p_{ball} = 0.060 \cdot 1
$$

$$
p_{ball} = 0.06 \text{ kg} \cdot \text{m/s}
$$

### Step 4: Compare the two values
Now we simply look at which magnitude is larger.

$$
0.06 > 0.02
$$

Therefore, $p_{ball} > p_{fly}$.

---

## Final Results Summary

**Momentum of the fly:**

$$
p_{fly} = 0.02 \text{ kg} \cdot \text{m/s}
$$

**Momentum of the tennis ball:**

$$
p_{ball} = 0.06 \text{ kg} \cdot \text{m/s}
$$

**Conclusion:** The **tennis ball** has greater momentum ($0.06$ compared to $0.02$). Even though the fly is moving much faster, the tennis ball's larger mass gives it more overall "quantity of motion."
