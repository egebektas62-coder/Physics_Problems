## 6. Instrument Precision

### Necessary Definitions and Formulas

#### 1. Least Count (Resolution)
The "least count" is the smallest value that an instrument can clearly display or measure. For a digital display, it is the place value of the final decimal digit shown on the screen.

#### 2. Absolute Uncertainty (Half-Digit Rule)
Based on the specific instructions of this problem, the absolute uncertainty ($\Delta T$) is calculated by dividing the least count by 2:

$$
\Delta T = \frac{\text{Least Count}}{2}
$$

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

Let's identify the given parameters from the problem:
* Temperature Reading ($T$): **25.4°C**

#### Step 1: Determine the Least Count
Look at the final digit of the reading (**25.4**). The last digit is a "4", which sits in the "tenths" decimal place. Therefore, the smallest increment this specific thermometer can measure (its least count) is **0.1°C**.

#### Step 2: Calculate the Absolute Uncertainty
Divide the least count by two, as instructed by the problem:

$$
\Delta T = \frac{0.1}{2}
$$

$$
\Delta T = 0.05\text{ °C}
$$

#### Step 3: Decimal Matching (Crucial Step for Professors)
Here is a very important detail that professors look for in lab reports: The uncertainty dictates the decimal places of the final answer. Because our calculated uncertainty ($0.05$) reaches the "hundredths" place, we must mathematically add a placeholder zero to our original measurement so they match perfectly. 

Therefore, our base reading of **25.4** must be written as **25.40**.

---

### Final Result

The absolute uncertainty of this measurement is **0.05°C**. 

The full measurement should be correctly reported as:

$$
T = (25.40 \pm 0.05)\text{ °C}
$$
