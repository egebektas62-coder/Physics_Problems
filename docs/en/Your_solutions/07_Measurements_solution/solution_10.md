## 10. Light Speed Measurement

### Necessary Definitions and Formulas

#### 1. Standing Waves and Wavelength
Inside a microwave oven, the electromagnetic waves bounce back and forth to create a standing wave. The melted spots on the chocolate occur at the antinodes (points of maximum energy). The distance ($d$) between two adjacent melted spots represents exactly half of a wavelength ($\lambda$).

$$
\lambda = 2 \cdot d
$$

#### 2. The Wave Equation (Speed of Light)
The speed of any wave ($c$) is the product of its frequency ($f$) and its wavelength ($\lambda$).

$$
c = f \cdot \lambda
$$

#### 3. Percentage Error
To compare our experimental result with the accepted theoretical value of light speed, we calculate the percentage error:

$$
\text{Percentage Error} = \frac{|c_{experimental} - c_{accepted}|}{c_{accepted}} \cdot 100
$$

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

Let's identify the parameters for this experiment. *(Note: The measured distance $d$ is an experimental example)*.
* Microwave Frequency ($f$): $2.45\text{ GHz} = 2.45 \times 10^9\text{ Hz}$
* Measured Distance between spots ($d$): $6.0\text{ cm} = 0.06\text{ m}$
* Accepted Speed of Light ($c_{accepted}$): $300,000,000\text{ m/s}$

#### Step 1: Calculate the Wavelength ($\lambda$)
Multiply the measured distance between the melted spots by 2 to find the full wavelength of the microwave.

$$
\lambda = 2 \cdot 0.06
$$

$$
\lambda = 0.12\text{ m}
$$

#### Step 2: Calculate the Experimental Speed of Light ($c_{experimental}$)
Multiply the microwave's frequency by the calculated wavelength.

$$
c_{experimental} = (2.45 \times 10^9) \cdot 0.12
$$

$$
c_{experimental} = 294,000,000\text{ m/s}
$$

#### Step 3: Calculate the Percentage Error
Compare the experimental speed with the accepted value to find the error margin.

$$
\text{Percentage Error} = \frac{|294,000,000 - 300,000,000|}{300,000,000} \cdot 100
$$

$$
\text{Percentage Error} = \frac{6,000,000}{300,000,000} \cdot 100
$$

$$
\text{Percentage Error} = 0.02 \cdot 100
$$

$$
\text{Percentage Error} = 2.0\%
$$

---

### Final Results Summary

* **Calculated Wavelength:** $0.12\text{ m}$
* **Experimental Speed of Light:** $294,000,000\text{ m/s}$
* **Percentage Error:** $2.0\%$

**Conclusion:** The experimental result is extremely close to the accepted value of $300,000,000\text{ m/s}$. A $2.0\%$ error is highly accurate for a home-based measurement using a simple ruler and food items, demonstrating the validity of the wave equation.
