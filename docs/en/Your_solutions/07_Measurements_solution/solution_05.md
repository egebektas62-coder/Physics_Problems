## 5. Percentage Calculation

### Necessary Definitions and Formulas

#### 1. Percentage (Relative) Uncertainty
To find the percentage uncertainty, you divide the absolute uncertainty ($\Delta t$) by the measured base value ($t$) and multiply by 100. This tells you exactly what fraction of your total measurement the error represents.

$$
\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{\Delta t}{t} \right) \cdot 100
$$

---

### Step-by-Step Solution

Let's identify the given parameters from the problem:
* Measured Time ($t$): 5.45 s
* Absolute Uncertainty ($\Delta t$): 0.22 s

#### Step 1: Calculate the Fractional Error
Substitute the given values into the formula:

$$
\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{0.22}{5.45} \right) \cdot 100
$$

$$
\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = (0.040366...) \cdot 100
$$

#### Step 2: Convert to Percentage
Multiply by 100 to shift the decimal place and get the raw percentage:

$$
\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = 4.0366...
$$

#### Step 3: Significant Figures and Final Rounding
In error analysis, we must respect the precision of our starting numbers. The given absolute uncertainty (0.22) has exactly two significant figures. Therefore, it is mathematically correct to round our final percentage uncertainty to two significant figures as well. 

4.0366... rounds perfectly down to **4.0**.

---

### Final Result

The percentage uncertainty of the time measurement is **4.0%**.
